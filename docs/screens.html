What to look at

  1. Six states, six windows. IN, ARMED, WARMING, BLOCKED, HALTED, IDLE. The badge and the footer always agree, so the state is readable from across the desk.
  2. The z-strip is the one non-numeric thing on the window: a 15px rule showing where z sits between the two entry thresholds.
  3. Bands are shown as prices (LO −2 / HI +2), not only as a z, because you check a level against the market.
  4. A blocked signal says why in words and names the number that failed — sigma 0.42 against a 0.60 round trip.
  5. Stale kills the z, not the close. Brent shows an em dash rather than a last-known value, and CLOSE NOW stays live.
  6. Statistics run with the algo off (WTI) — otherwise you cannot judge whether to turn it on.
  7. UNCLAIMED is a red banner with two buttons and closes nothing by itself.
  8. UAT is stamped top-right in amber; PROD is the same badge in red.
  9. The z-strip: keep it, or numbers only? You said no graphs — I read this as an indicator, not a chart, but it is the one thing on the window that is not a figure.
  10. Window size is ~270×415, so eight fit a 1920×1080 desk with room. Bigger figures and fewer windows, or as-is?

What to look at

  1. Six groups, ported from the Stat-Arb settings but scoped to one contract: signal, filters, size & risk, execution, costs, contract.
  2. Every cost input is on the same card as the number it produces — round trip, break-even, target — so a change is checked where it is made.
  3. Exit threshold greys out in profit mode rather than disappearing, so you can see what the other mode would use.
  4. Venue-derived specs are labelled. Typing over one is an override and the window carries a mark.
  5. Profit target basis — margin, notional, or sigma at entry? I have defaulted to initial margin, which is what MT5-Trader uses. Margin needs the venue to report it; if Orient does not, notional is the reliable one.
  6. Limit entries add re-pricing, timeouts and queue handling. Worth it, or should the algo only ever cross with market/IOC?

What to look at

  1. The credential fields are FIX session fields — comp ids, sequence reset, dictionary — because that is what connecting here means.
  2. Environment is a radio, not a checkbox, and PROD is a separate venue row with its own credentials.
  3. Diagnose speaks the venue's words and every failure carries the step that fixes it, offered as a button rather than applied silently.
  4. A tick mismatch is a FAIL, not a warning — every money figure runs through it.
  5. Contracts table doubles as the health list: OPEN, NO DATA, TICK MISMATCH.
  6. Symbol identification. I have assumed one Symbol (55) names the spread. If Orient uses SecurityID + SecurityExchange, or a multi-leg definition, this table grows two columns — do you know which?
  7. Separate market-data session? The field is there and blank means "same session". Worth knowing before the wiring phase.

What to look at

  1. One contract at a time, by default. A blended win rate across eight contracts tells you nothing about which one to turn off — the sub-tab switches, and All contracts is the comparison.
  2. The SD touch table answers a question rather than counting events: of the touches at this level, how many reverted, how long they took, and how far they went against you first. That is what says whether ±2.00 is right for this contract.
  3. No chart library. Counts carry an in-cell bar so they compare at a glance and stay numbers. The original tab used a Chart.js bar — this is the same information without the dependency.
  4. A touch still open is unresolved, not a failure, and is excluded from the percentage rather than counted as a miss.
  5. Costs are budget against measurement, and the finding is actionable: the slippage budget is 0.19 ticks generous, so the edge filter is refusing entries it would have paid for. One button applies it.
  6. On margin is carried per trade — the return on what the position actually locked up, which is the same base the profit target uses.
  7. Under 10 closed trades gets no verdict. Six losing trades is not evidence, and a page that says so is worth more than one that ranks noise.
  8. Scope of the SD study — touches are recorded whether or not the algo was on, so the table covers hours you were not trading. Right, or should it only count touches while the contract was armed?
  9. A threshold sweep — "what would 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 have done on this contract" is computable from the same series. Worth a panel here, or is that the backtest, later?

The things I need from you

Everything above is drawn to a default I picked. These are the ones where a different answer changes what gets built, roughly in the order they bite.

  1. The profit target is a percentage of what? initial margin / notional / sigma at entry — margin is what the trade costs to hold and is what MT5-Trader uses, but it needs Orient to report margin per contract. Notional always works. Sigma expresses the target in the move being caught.
  2. Does the algo rest limit entries, or only cross? Limits save the spread but bring re-pricing, timeouts and queue handling. Market/IOC only is materially simpler and always fills.
  3. Should this screen be able to close a position at all? I have kept CLOSE NOW and KILL ALL as safety. If even that belongs in TT, the screen sends nothing but the algo's own orders.
  4. How many contracts on screen at once, typically? I have sized windows so eight fit 1920×1080. Sixteen means smaller figures; four means I can make them much bigger.
  5. Keep the z-strip? The 15px rule under the statistics. It is the only non-numeric element on a window.
  6. How is a spread contract identified at Orient? Symbol (55) alone, or SecurityID + SecurityExchange, or a multi-leg security definition? It changes the contract table and the security-definition request.
  7. Do SD touches count while the algo is off? They are recorded from the price series either way, so the study covers hours you were not trading. That is the honest sample of the contract's behaviour — but if you only want to judge the hours you were armed, say so and it becomes a filter.
  8. A threshold sweep on the Analysis tab — what 1.5 / 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 would have done on this contract's own recorded series. Computable from what we already store. Worth a panel now, or leave it to the backtest later?
  9. Is market data a separate FIX session? The field is drawn; knowing now saves a refactor at wiring time.
